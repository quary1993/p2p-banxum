How a loan gets onto this platform

Two origins, one structure.

Some loans we write ourselves. Others were written by a Romanian IFN months earlier and the claim was assigned to us, then to you. Either way you hold the claim against the company, and either way we collect it.

Where the two paths meet

The top row is the only part that differs. Everything below the line is identical — which is why these sit in the same list, with no badge and no warning.

Origin A · direct
We write the loan
We assess the company ourselves, the opportunity funds, the loan is created that day and assigned to the investors who funded it. Nobody has paid anything yet.
Origin B · purchased
An IFN wrote it, months ago
The IFN originated the loan and assigns the claim to Banxum, which assigns it on to you. It keeps at least 5% beside you and steps out of the chain.
From here, identical You hold the claim, and it is against the company
Banxum collects
Every payment comes to us and we pass it to you, on the same dates, in the same statement.
Banxum is security agent
We hold the charge over the collateral for you, and we are the ones who enforce it.
Same collateral rules
A margin set per project against an independent valuation, and penalty interest from the first missed day.
If an IFN went bust tomorrow, nothing in the box above changes. It is not in the chain — it is a company that happens to still own a slice next to you.

How a company gets onto this list
Registered with the BNR as an IFN, with the register number shown here so you can check it yourself.
At least 2 years of its own lending before we look at a single file, and its own arrears figures disclosed to us.
Keeps 5% or more of everything it sells here. That is the legal floor; 4 of the 5 chose to keep more.
We re-check the collateral ourselves — the valuation, the charge, the registration. We do not take the originator's word for it.
Meeting all 4 is not a judgement about whether its borrowers will pay. That is what the bars beside it are for.
A good record is not a guarantee, and 14 loans is not a record at all — the case counts sit next to every figure for that reason. Capital Leasing has 204 behind it and Prima Leasing has 14; those two numbers should not be read with the same confidence.
